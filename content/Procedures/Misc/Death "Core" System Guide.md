This guide explains the death and resurrection mechanics used on the Landfall server, including cores, shells, and the consequences of multiple deaths.

---

## 🧬 Core Fundamentals

In Landfall-830, death is no longer permanent. Nearly every character carries a **core**—an implanted consciousness anchor that preserves not just memories, but the essence of who you are. When your body dies, your core ensures you can return.

|Term|Description|
|---|---|
|**Core**|A device that records memory, personality, and soul-data. Nearly universal, though culturally controversial.|
|**Shell**|Your physical body. When you die and return, you get a new shell—not always identical to the original.|
|**Drop**|Temporary death. Your core remains intact and you respawn in a new shell. Standard for PvP deaths.|
|**Corebreak**|Permanent death. Occurs when the core is destroyed or disrupted. This results in a canon character death.|

## ⚡ Death and Resurrection

### How Death Works

1. **You die in-game** (PvP, PvE, fall damage, etc.)
2. **Your core preserves your consciousness**
3. **You "boot" into a new shell**
4. **You continue playing** with potential RP consequences

> 💡 _Drop counters are tracked server-side. You can reference your Drop count in-character, but numbers aren't globally visible unless roleplayed._

### Shell Traits

Each time you Drop and return in a new shell, you may experience:

- Minor stat variations (speed, jump, resistance, strength)
- Personality quirks or physical anomalies
- Memory gaps or trauma from the death experience
- **Genetic Echoes**: Your new shell retains encoded genetic data from your original body. Implanted cores manipulate genes to this end.

## 🔄 Advanced Mechanics

|Mechanic|Description|RP Impact|
|---|---|---|
|**Booting**|Slang for coming back after a Drop. Auto-recovery in civilized regions.|May fail in blackout zones or due to sabotage|
|**Slipboot**|Warping your core data to another location/planet via portal, creating a new shell and core|Used for long-distance travel or medical emergencies|
|**Burn Years**|How long your core has been active across all shells|Characters can be centuries old across multiple bodies|
|**Decay**|Degradation after many Burn Years and Drops|Causes memory loss, faction exile, or final death arcs|

## ⚖️ In-World Consequences

### Legal and Social Impact

- **Drop Zones** are regulated in many territories
- Causing unauthorized Drops may be prosecuted as attempted murder
- Dying repeatedly damages your reputation and social standing
- Some regions treat all unauthorized Drops as "soul-violence"

### Cultural Perspectives

Different factions view cores differently:

- Some governments register cores as **state property**
- Religious extremists see cores as **soul-binding sacrilege**
- Anti-Transhumanists debate whether you're still **truly human** after multiple Drops
- **Coreless characters** (rare) risk permanent death but may be treated with awe or fear

## 💭 Roleplay Guidelines

### Questions to Consider

- How does your character feel about their core?
- Do they remember every death clearly, or are there gaps?
- After multiple Drops, do they still feel like themselves?
- Would they kill someone knowing their core is intact? What if it's not?

### Death Still Matters

Even with cores, death has consequences:

- Physical trauma and psychological scars
- Lost possessions or territory
- Damaged relationships and trust
- Acceleration of Decay

> 🎭 _Remember: The core system allows PvP without derailing your story, but death should still feel significant in your roleplay. To this end, **all** drops are canon and are **not** reversible. Players are treated as being in-character at all times._
